About this project

What it is

A full client proposal of the kind a private bank prepares: a balance sheet review, an investment policy statement, a plan to unwind a concentrated stock position, a diversified portfolio built on J.P. Morgan's published 2027 market forecasts, and a retirement simulation. The family is fictional, but every market input is real and sourced.

Why I built it

I'm recruiting for wealth management and private banking roles, and I wanted to practice the core of the job: turning a family's situation into a plan they can understand and follow. The art collection section draws on my summer researching the Korean art market at Seoul Auction Blue.

Real-world evidence

Concentration is risky even in good companies. Studying every US stock since 1926, finance professor Hendrik Bessembinder found that most individual stocks earned less than one-month Treasury bills over their lifetimes, and that about 4% of companies account for all of the stock market's net wealth creation. Holding one stock is a bet on being in that 4%.

Sources: Bessembinder, "Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?", Journal of Financial Economics, 2018; J.P. Morgan Asset Management, 2027 Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions.

The family: high income, rich on paper, exposed to one chip company

Daniel Han, 54, is a senior vice president at Meridian Semiconductor, a fictional listed chipmaker. He earns about $650K in salary and bonus plus roughly $800K a year in stock grants. Grace Han, 52, trained in gallery work and manages the family's collection of Dansaekhwa and contemporary Korean painting. They have two children, 17 and 14, and live in New York.

Their goals: retire at 62 without cutting their lifestyle, pay for college, help the children later in life, and give to Korean arts education.

Daniel's career, his annual bonus and $6.0M of their savings all depend on the same company. If the semiconductor cycle turns, all three get hit together.

Four problems, in order of how much damage they can do

1. Concentration

49% of liquid wealth in one stock with roughly 35% annual volatility. A 40% fall would take $2.4M off the plan, likely in the same year Daniel's bonus shrinks.

2. Embedded gains

Cost basis is about $1.2M on $6.0M. Selling everything at once triggers about $1.1M of federal tax. The tax cost is the reason they haven't sold.

3. Retirement funding

They need about $480K a year after tax, starting in 8 years and lasting possibly 35 years. That needs reliable growth and a cash reserve so they never sell in a downturn.

4. Illiquid assets

The art and the home make up 33% of net worth. The art is not covered by any insurance schedule, has no current appraisal, and the will doesn't say who gets it.

Investment policy statement, in brief

The policy sets the rules before markets test them. Every recommendation in this proposal follows from it.

Required return: about 3.6% a year in nominal terms just to fund the spending plan to age 95 in a straight-line projection. The proposed portfolio is expected to compound at 7.4% under J.P. Morgan's assumptions. That leaves room for bad sequences.

Return objective
Fund $480K/yr after-tax spending from 62, inflation-adjusted, with a ≥ 90% simulated success rate; grow the surplus for the children.
Risk tolerance
Above average in ability (large surplus, long horizon), moderate in willingness. Grace was uncomfortable with the 2022 drawdown. Portfolio volatility target is about 10%.
Time horizon
Two stages: 8 years of saving, then 30+ years of spending.
Liquidity
12 months of spending in cash now; two years ($1.2M) in T-bills and short munis by retirement. Tuition comes from the 529 plans.
Taxes
Top federal bracket. Bonds held in the taxable account go into municipal bonds. Gains are realized gradually across tax years, and losses are harvested.
Legal and regulatory
Daniel is a corporate insider. All sales go through a Rule 10b5-1 plan, which has a 90-day cooling-off period, and are pre-cleared with the company's general counsel.
Unique circumstances
Concentrated employer stock, an art collection, and a wish to give to Korean arts education.

The stock plan: four tools, staged over three tax years

Selling everything at once wastes money on tax. Holding everything keeps the risk. The plan splits the $6.0M position by the job each tool does best.

Art and estate: treat the collection as an asset, not décor

  • Appraise and insure. Get a qualified appraisal now and every 3–5 years, list each work on a fine-art insurance schedule, and keep provenance files.
  • Know the tax. Gains on sold art are taxed as collectibles at up to 28%, more than stocks. Donating it or leaving it in the estate usually beats selling.
  • Use it for liquidity. An art-secured credit line can cover a cash need during a trading blackout instead of a forced stock sale.
  • Name the heirs. Leave specific works to specific people in the will, and consider leaving one work to a museum.

Estate math

The 2026 federal estate and gift exemption is $15M per person ($30M per couple). It no longer has an expiry date and is indexed to inflation from 2027. At $17.2M, the Hans are under the federal line, so the focus shifts:

  • New York has its own estate tax with a much lower threshold, so state-level planning comes first.
  • Annual exclusion gifts of $19,000 per donor per child, or $76K/yr in total, move growth out of the estate.
  • The low-basis shares they keep get a step-up in basis at death, so there is little tax reason to give them away during life.

Implementation and monitoring

  • Rebalancing: each asset class within ±5 points of target (±20% of target for small sleeves), checked quarterly. Use new cash before selling anything.
  • Asset location: high yield, hedge funds and taxable bonds go in the 401(k)/IRA; munis and equity index funds go in the taxable account.
  • Tax-loss harvesting in the taxable account to offset gains from the stock sales.
  • Benchmark: a blended index at policy weights, net of fees.
  • Spending guardrail: if the portfolio falls 20% below plan, trim spending 10% until it recovers. This is the main defense in the skeptic case.

  • Concentrated case: 49% stock and the rest in a 60/30/10 mix of US large cap, Treasuries and cash, starting at $12.2M with no sale tax paid.
